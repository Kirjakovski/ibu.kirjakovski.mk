## Lecture Summary:

Between-subjects designs assign each participant to only one condition, ensuring groups remain independent. In basic versions, subjects are randomly assigned to specific levels of a single independent variable (IV), such as an experimental or control group. Differences between groups are analyzed using independent-samples t-tests or one-way ANOVAs. This design eliminates carry-over effects but requires large samples to overcome between-person variability.

Factorial designs involve two or more IVs where every level is crossed, assigning participants to exactly one cell. A 2×2 design involves four groups, while a 2×3 involves six; the total cells equal the product of all factor levels. These designs reveal main effects for each IV and interaction effects, which show if one IV’s impact depends on another level. Significant interactions require cautious interpretation of main effects and follow-up via simple effects analyses. For example, a 2×2×3 notation indicates three factors totaling 12 cells. While between-subjects factorials prevent order confounds, they're participant-intensive; a 3×4 design with 20 people per cell needs 240 individuals. so, researchers often use mixed factorial designs to boost statistical power.
