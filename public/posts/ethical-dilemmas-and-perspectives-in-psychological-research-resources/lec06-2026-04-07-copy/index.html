#07: Research and Legal Systems

ETHICAL DILEMMAS AND PERSPECTIVES IN PSYCHOLOGICAL RESEARCH
07 Apr 2026

Lecture Summary:

Psychology has long been organised around codes of professional ethics. What is less often emphasised in psychology curricula is that, in many jurisdictions, the obligations articulated in those codes are simultaneously codified in domestic law. In Macedonia, the practice of psychology is regulated by a layered legal framework whose violation carries professional, administrative, and in certain cases criminal consequences. Treating ethics and law as separate spheres therefore mischaracterises the operating environment of psychologists.

The principal instrument governing professional practice is the Law on the Performance of Psychological Practice (Закон за вршење на психолошка дејност; Ministry of Health), which establishes the Chamber of Psychologists of Macedonia (Комора на психолози) as the licensing authority for both general and special competencies . Adjacent statutes regulate substantive areas of practice. The Law on Mental Health defines patient rights, the criteria for involuntary hospitalisation, and the standards for valid consent in populations with mental health conditions. The Law on Personal Data Protection brings Macedonian data-protection law into substantial alignment with the European Union’s General Data Protection Regulation and treats psychological data as a special category requiring heightened safeguards, supplies a criminal-law backstop, criminalising the unauthorised disclosure of professional secrets and the falsification of expert reports that are of direct relevance to psychologists serving as forensic expert witnesses or maintaining clinical records.

In routine practice, these instruments translate into a set of concrete procedural obligations. A researcher undertaking a study at a Macedonian institution is required to obtain ethical clearance from the relevant institutional review body, document informed consent in a manner consistent with the Law on Personal Data Protection, and ensure that data-minimisation and anonymisation principles are observed throughout analysis and dissemination. A practicing clinician must hold a current Chamber licence, document consent for assessment and intervention, and observe the statutory limits of confidentiality, which yield to disclosure obligations under conditions of imminent risk to life or by judicial order. A persistent challenge in this and comparable jurisdictions is the unevenness of enforcement across small private practices and unaffiliated research projects. This implementation gap does not diminish the binding force of the legal framework but it strengthens the case for explicit instruction in legal compliance throughout psychological training. Where professional ethics articulate a normative ideal, the law specifies the institutional consequences of departing from it.
