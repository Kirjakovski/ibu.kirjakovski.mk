#07: Conformity and Obedience

SOCIAL PSYCHOLOGY
17 Apr 2026

Lecture Summary:

Social influence manifests through persuasion, compliance, conformity, and obedience. Persuasion actively targets beliefs via communication, while compliance involves behavioral changes following requests, often without genuine internal agreement. Conformity occurs when individuals adopt group norms to fit in, and obedience results from direct orders by authority figures.

People conform due to informational social influence, using others for guidance in ambiguous crises or expert settings, or normative social influence to gain acceptance. Sherif’s autokinetic studies demonstrated private acceptance through group consensus, while Baron found higher conformity when accuracy importance increased. Conversely, Asch’s line-judgment studies showed people knowingly provide wrong answers to match a unanimous group. Conformity risk grows with group importance and size, though having an ally or belonging to an individualistic culture reduces this pressure. Minorities may influence majorities through informational means. While injunctive norms define social approval, descriptive norms define actual behavior; however, the latter can unintentionally encourage negative behavioral shifts. Techniques like foot-in-the-door further manipulate behavior.

Obedience remains the most potent influence, as seen in Milgram’s experiments where 62.5% of participants delivered maximum shocks under authority. Compliance dropped when the victim was closer, the setting was less formal, or dissenters were present. People obey by following established norms, justifying incremental escalations, and offloading personal responsibility to authorities. Ultimately, these studies reveal how intense situational pressures, rather than innate malice, drive ordinary individuals to perform harmful acts.
