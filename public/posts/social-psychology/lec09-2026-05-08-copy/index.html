#08: Aggression

SOCIAL PSYCHOLOGY
24 Apr 2026

Lecture Summary:

Aggression is intentional behavior aimed at causing physical or psychological harm, regardless of success. Hostile aggression stems from anger, whereas instrumental aggression serves as a tool for other goals. Violence represents extreme aggression, such as murder or assault. Debate continues over whether aggression is innate or learned. Evolutionarily, men physically aggressed to protect groups and status, while women did so to protect offspring. Research shows experience can override instinct, though biological patterns remain. High testosterone levels correlate with aggression, though behavior also influences hormone levels.

Cultural norms, like the “culture of honor,” shape aggressive responses to insults. Men typically use physical aggression, while women favor relational aggression like gossip or cyberbullying. Frustration-aggression theory suggests goal interference triggers responses, especially through relative deprivation. Provocation often leads to retaliation unless people consider intent or costs. Remarkably, the “weapons effect” shows that merely seeing a gun increases aggression. Physical factors like alcohol, heat, or pain also lower self-control. Sexual assault, often by acquaintances, follows learned social “sexual scripts.”

Media violence, particularly interactive games, correlates with hostile thoughts and emotional numbing. Reducing aggression requires detailed approaches, as harsh punishment often fuels a cycle of violence. Catharsis is ineffective; instead, calming techniques, communication training, and empathy are more successful. School programs like the Olweus Bullying Prevention Program use clear limits and positive modeling. Ultimately, aggression emerges from a complex mix of biology and environment, requiring multi-level interventions.
