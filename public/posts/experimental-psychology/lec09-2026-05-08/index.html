#09: The Basic of Experimentation: Within-Subjects Designs

EXPERIMENTAL PSYCHOLOGY
08 May 2026

Lecture Summary:

In experimental psychology, signals get drowned out by individual differences. One participant reacts strongly to a manipulation, another barely at all, and between-person variability swallows the effect you care about. Within-subjects designs (or repeated-measures, depending on your textbook) are the standard response: every participant goes through every condition, each serving as their own control. The payoff is statistical power. When the same participant contributes scores across conditions, between-person variance gets partitioned out of the error term, leaving the within-person change you wanted to detect. Experiments therefore need fewer participants. A four-condition study requiring around sixty between-subjects can sometimes run with fifteen within-subjects, depending on how large the individual differences were that you just cancelled out.

But there are some costs. Long sessions wear participants down; attention drifts, motivation decays, late trials look noisier than early ones. Presentation order can also confound results, because people get faster, slower, or more bored with practice regardless of the manipulation. Carryover is the ugliest version: a treatment doesn’t just leave a memory trace but can change what the participant is able to perceive or do next. Sniffing gasoline before evaluating a perfume is the usual intuition pump. Counterbalancing handles part of this. Varying condition order across participants—reverse counterbalancing (A-B-B-A) for two conditions, a Latin square for more—distributes order and practice effects across the design. The catch is that this only works when carryover is symmetric. If A changes how participants respond to B but B doesn’t equivalently affect A, a balanced Lating square scheme helps you. The options then are a between-subjects design, washout periods long enough for carryover to decay, or modelling the asymmetry in the analysis.

The choice between within and between is practical, not principled—depending on recruitment capacity, likely treatment interactions, and how much effect is hiding inside individual differences. When conditions allow it, letting each participant be their own baseline remains one of the cheapest ways to pull a small effect from noisy data.
