#07: The Basic of Experimentation

EXPERIMENTAL PSYCHOLOGY
17 Apr 2026

Lecture Summary:

Psychology experiments center on two variables: the independent variable (IV), which researchers manipulate, like administering drugs or varying exposure, and the dependent variable (DV), which they measure. Trustworthy studies require operationalization, turning abstract concepts like anxiety into observable procedures. Measurements must also be reliable, providing consistent results across time and raters, and valid, capturing the intended construct. Construct validity occurs when a measure correlates specifically with related criteria. Internal validity ensures only the IV caused changes in the DV. Uncontrolled variables, or confounds, prevent causal conclusions. Common threats include history, maturation, statistical regression, and attrition. Researchers counter these by eliminating extraneous factors, standardizing conditions, and using random assignment to distribute individual differences evenly.

When randomization is insufficient, researchers use matching, hold characteristics constant, or include potential confounds as additional IVs. Within-subjects designs require addressing order effects through counterbalancing like Latin Squares. Minimizing human bias is also critical. Participants might alter behavior based on demand characteristics, or experimenters may influence results through the Rosenthal effect. To control these, researchers employ single-blind or double-blind procedures, or use automated data collection.
